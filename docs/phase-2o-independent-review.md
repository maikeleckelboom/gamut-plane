## Architecture and source review

Authored `ColorValue` remains the only color authority. Accepted state remains selection/Status/Boundary/Reference. Geometry owns projection and domain constraints. Render owns the camera, requested/presented scheduling barrier, visible-window sampling and cache identity. UI owns wheel/pan/keyboard arbitration through structural ports. Adapters own committed resources and coherent DOM presentation. Camera state stays ephemeral and absent from public instrument state.

The tracer split removes display policy from core rather than documenting the violation. Core's internal sibling entry exports numerical crossings/intervals; render owns `perceptualGuides.ts`, its normalized tolerance, four bounded memo slots and caller-owned buffers. Render's independent conversion oracle uses the existing pinned `@texel/color` as a dev dependency only. Generated gamut data and exact analysis were not changed.

The numerical review covered cubic derivative splitting, safeguarded roots, membership transitions, tangencies, coincident constraints, black/white degeneracy, notch exit/entry/exit ordering, traversal checks and failure distinctions. Tangency without a membership change should not create an interval boundary. Roots are floating-point solutions, not exact arithmetic. The chroma search ceiling and finite event seeding still depend on the supported-gamut domain; they are not a general topology certificate. A discovered disconnected loop causes explicit failure rather than omission, but an undiscovered component remains the proof gap.

No broader framework rewrite was warranted. VueUse remains appropriate for Vue's generic mounted listeners/resize/DPR; domain ownership, cancellation and camera policy stay shared and framework-neutral. Gesture preview restoration now belongs to the gesture owner instead of duplicating its pending point in adapters. Pan stores its initiating normalized point, removing the cached client-pixel extent; adapters keep measurement invalidation. Deduplication happens once at the actual Float32 output boundary. Test fixtures dispose mounted viewport controllers to avoid global-listener contamination.

Owner-level regressions were added or strengthened in UI's `viewportInteraction.test.ts`, the paired `planeViewport` adapter suites, core's numerical `boundaryTrace.test.ts`, and render's `fieldViewportRaster.test.ts`, `perceptualGuides.test.ts` and `tracedGuides.test.ts`. The web viewport suite adds one real ancestor-scroll/capture regression. Existing owner tests were retained for controls, transform inversion, rejected/accepted geometry, camera silence, cache identity, resources, Strict Mode and hydration. One pan assertion formerly compared raw floating-point log strings; it now checks the two numeric displacements and resulting presented center independently at 1e-12 precision.

No measured normal-slice violation of 1 px was found; absence of such a violation does not satisfy continuous certification.

## Acceptance judgment

**Not acceptable against the current Phase 2O contract.** The repaired camera and input paths have substantially stronger evidence, and sampled contour accuracy is good. However, §16.1 requires continuous two-way accuracy for every successful guide, and the implementation still has no conservative error/completeness certificate. Documentation now states this gap instead of accepting the previous reports as proof. Completing that certificate or obtaining an explicit product decision to change the contract remains necessary; this review did not silently lower the requirement.

Physical Mac/Option-wheel and trackpad behavior, touch hardware, manual screen-reader use and cross-engine transcendental reproducibility remain unverified. The automated browser matrix is pinned Chromium. Hue/Lightness slider guide intervals retain the pre-existing sampled table; Chroma intervals and perceptual spatial Reference share the numerical ray crossings used by the new field contours. This review does not certify those legacy slider intervals to the field's magnified accuracy target, arbitrary future RGB gamuts, or malformed third-party geometry. Near-endpoint explicit guide failure is a known numerical limitation. The continuous error/completeness claims, rather than the previous acceptance report's confidence, remain the part I do not trust.
